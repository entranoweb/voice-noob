# Clip consent, provenance, and `forget`

A clip is a recording of a person. This page says what the bank records about each clip, what the tool refuses to do with a clip that lacks the record, and how a clip is removed everywhere this tool can reach.

Nothing here identifies a speaker. Every reference is **given** by a person and **opaque**: the tool never listens to a clip to work out who spoke, never matches voices, and never stores a voice embedding. The key that links an opaque id to a real person is kept by you, outside the bank.

## What each manifest field means

Added to `manifest.csv`, all optional. `clipbank build` writes them from what you give it (`--provenance`, `--speaker-ref`, `--lawful-basis`, `--consent-ref`, `--recorded-on`, `--retain-until`, `--call-fields`). Existing banks need no change to load.

| Column | Meaning | Allowed values |
|---|---|---|
| `provenance` | Is this a recording of a person, or a made voice? | `real`, or `synthetic` (a description may follow the word). `real-call` is read as `real`. Empty or anything else is **unknown** |
| `source_kind` | What the recording came from | `call`, `session` (a recording session) |
| `source_ref` | Its id on the platform it came from | An id of 3 to 80 characters, no spaces; not an address or a number. `build` fills it with the call id |
| `speaker_ref` | Who spoke, as an id | `spk_` then 8 to 32 lowercase letters and digits, at least one of each. Never a name or a phone number |
| `lawful_basis` | The UK GDPR Article 6 basis **and** the Article 9 condition you rely on to hold and use the recording. **Required on a real clip** | Any non-empty text. The tool does not check which basis applies; you decide it per clip source |
| `consent_ref` | Where the consent record is held. **Required on a real clip** | An id of a record kept elsewhere (a pointer, never the record) |
| `recorded_on` | The day it was recorded | `YYYY-MM-DD` |
| `retain_until` | The last day the clip may be used | `YYYY-MM-DD`, not before `recorded_on` |
| `label_confirmed_by` | Who listened and confirmed what the clip says | `rev_` then 8 to 32 random lowercase letters and digits. Needs `label_confirmed_on` |
| `label_confirmed_on` | When they did | `YYYY-MM-DD` |

The older `checked_by_human` column still works and still means "a person confirmed this label". A clip counts as confirmed if either it or `label_confirmed_by` is filled in.

The id rules cannot stop someone typing `spk_jane1985`. Mint ids at random and keep the mapping to people somewhere else.

**Special category.** Every real caller's clip (`provenance` real) is treated as special-category data: it is a voice, and it can carry what a patient says about their health. UK GDPR Article 9 needs a condition as well as an Article 6 basis, and the owner records both in `lawful_basis`. The loader marks each real clip `special_category: true` (a synthetic clip is not). **A real clip, or one whose provenance is blank or unrecognised (before this rule a blank meant real), with no `lawful_basis` or no `consent_ref` is refused** by the clip bank, the one gate every playback goes through, so `batch`, `run`, chains, regrade and the phone leg (`synthiq-caller-phone`, real and `--dry-run`) all obey it, and `validate` fails on it. The one way past is `--allow-missing-consent`, a switch of its own that `--allow-unknown-provenance` never implies (a clip of unknown provenance needs both fields too, or both flags); a call played that way stores `consent_missing` listing the clips, and `validate` says `CONSENT RECORD MISSING`. A clip that says `synthetic` needs neither field. `report`, `scorecard` and the acceptance page say "consent record missing" for any call played that way. `ingest` (P7) writes `lawful_basis` and `consent_ref` as empty columns and prints a line telling you to fill them: those clips are real, so a batch, run or phone call refuses them until both are filled. **Not in the tool, and a decision for the owner:** which basis and condition apply. Using a recording of a caller as test audio is also a different purpose from the call it was made for, so it is not enough that the call was lawful; decide the basis with whoever advises you on data protection. This page is not legal advice.

### What the tool does with them

- **`validate`** (with `--clips`) fails when a clip is past `retain_until` or a field is written and wrong. It also fails on a real clip with no `lawful_basis` or `consent_ref` (unless `--allow-missing-consent`). It reports, without failing, each clip with no provenance and each real recording that lacks a speaker ref or a date. A scenario that names a clip past retention is marked `CLIPS PAST RETENTION`; one that names a clip with unknown provenance says so.
- **`batch` and `run`** will not play a clip past its retention date (no flag allows it; a `retain_until` that cannot be read counts as past, and the day is judged in UTC). The check is made where every clip is played (the clip bank), so `batch`, `run`, chains, regrade, and the phone leg (`synthiq-caller-phone`, real and `--dry-run`) all obey it; the phone command takes `--allow-unknown-provenance` and `--clips-extra` too. They will not play a clip whose provenance is unknown unless given `--allow-unknown-provenance`; the call is then stored with `provenance_unknown` listing the clips, and `report` and `acceptance` say "provenance unknown", the same way they say "labels unverified". A clip that is not in any manifest has unknown provenance.
- A clip in an old manifest, or one with no `provenance` cell, is *unknown*, not *real*. Before this change the bank treated a missing value as real. To play an existing bank without the flag, add the column: `provenance` is `real` for recordings of people and `synthetic` for made voices.

## `forget`

```
synthiq-caller forget <clip id | spk_ref> --clips <bank> [--clips-extra <bank>]
    [--scenarios <dir>]... [--results <dir>]...
    --operator op_<opaque id> [--reason erasure_request|consent_withdrawn|retention_expired|other]
    [--request-ref <id>] [--keep-text] [--recordings <dir>]... [--dry-run]
```

Run it with `--dry-run` first: it lists everything below and changes nothing, not even the log.

It takes one clip id (with or without an audio ending; `a` never touches `a.v2`), or a speaker ref, which means every clip with that ref in any manifest or earlier removal record. Then, for each clip:

The target is never used as a pattern: `*`, `?` and `[ab]` are refused, and it must be a clip in a manifest, an earlier removal record, or an audio file of exactly that name, or a known speaker ref. Anything else changes nothing.

1. **Audio.** Deletes `<clip>.wav`, `.mp3` or `.flac` (exact name) from every bank directory, including its subfolders. A symlinked clip is resolved: its target is deleted too when it is inside the bank and is a clip being forgotten. A link to another clip (another speaker's, say) loses only the link: `forget` says `target <path> belongs to clip <id>, not removed`. A target outside the bank is left, `forget` says `not deleted: <path> is outside the bank`, the removal is `incomplete` and the command exits 1; delete it yourself if the request covers it. **Ingested calls.** When the bank is an `ingest` case's `clips` folder, the whole call it was cut from goes with the clip: `audio.*` is deleted, and so is every other file a capture writes except `capture.json` (`transcript.txt`, `conversation.json` and `trace.json`, which hold what the person said and the call's tool arguments; the list is `capture._written_names()`, so a file added to capture is covered). With `--keep-text` those files are kept and listed (the audio still goes). `capture.json` holds identifiers, times, hashes and the audio file name, no words of the person, and stays.
2. **Manifest.** Removes the clip's row from each `manifest.csv` and its entry from `overrides.json`, because both hold a transcript of what the person said.
3. **Scenarios and chains** in each `--scenarios` directory that play it. A persona fact a removed clip answered (the step's `field`) is blanked; any other persona fact is left, because it may hold what the caller said: `forget` lists each such scenario file under NOT removed with "remove or edit persona facts by hand". The step's clip becomes `missing/<clip>` and the scenario gets a `missing_clips` entry saying the clip was removed. From then on `validate` prints `MISSING CLIP` for it and a batch lists it under "not run: missing clips". JSON files are rewritten in the indent they had, YAML/YML files (as `batch` loads them, needs PyYAML) are rewritten as YAML; key order is kept. A scenario that names the clip and cannot be read or rewritten is reported and makes the run incomplete. Restore it by recording a new clip and editing the step back.
4. **Stored results** in each `--results` directory: every `run-*.json`, `phone-run-*.json`, `chain-*.json` and `batch.json` that names a clip (and any other `.json` whose `record` has the RunRecord shape, `scenario` and `said`; any other JSON that merely mentions a clip is listed as `mentions <clip>, not changed` and left alone) gets a top-level `clips_removed` list (`clip`, `removal`, `removed_on`). A chain file is marked when any run it points to was. **Erasure redaction.** Unless told otherwise, every stored run that played a removed clip loses its free text: the timeline's `heard` and `agent` text and `hangup` quote, `said` (`intended`, `heard`, `answering`, `dtmf`), `unanswered`, `ended`, the trace (`transcript`, tool-call `arguments`, `result`, `outputs`, `request`, `error`, `termination`), `trace_error`, `read_back` and its error, `hook_error`, and in the grade each check's `why` and the `fields` text; a check's `what` becomes its `label` (the check kind and the tool or field it is about, with no value: `field name reaches a tool as said`), so a forgotten failed call still shows which check failed and whether it passed. A result stored before labels existed has its `what` redacted whole. Also the `fields` values, and `reason`. Each becomes `[redacted <removal id>]`. These stay: the grade outcome and fault class, whether each check passed, every timing, tool and step names, clip ids. The mark lists exactly the fields that were redacted (`redacted_fields`), and `forget` prints the same list and what was kept. A chain result that points to one of those runs is redacted the same way: the captured `variables` keep their names and lose their values (they come from the trace), and the chain's `reason` and each call's `reason` are redacted (`variables`, `reason`, `calls[*].reason` in `redacted_fields`); its outcomes and run links stay. `batch.json` is marked only. A call that comes after a forgotten one in the same chain was given the values that call captured (an appointment id, say), so it is marked and redacted the same way. `regrade` and `regrade --chains` skip any result carrying a `clips_removed` mark, kept text included, and keep its stored grade (the scenario has been rewritten since, so a new grade would not be that call's); they print "N results of removed clips not regraded". `--keep-text` keeps the text, is allowed only with `--reason retention_expired`, and the message says nothing was redacted. (Default pending owner review.) The grade is not touched, so a result stays what it was; `report` adds "clips removed: N call(s)" so nobody mistakes it for something that can be played again.
5. **The record.** Appends two lines to `removals.jsonl`, `started` before anything is changed and `complete` or `incomplete` (with a `failed` list) when it ends, in the `--clips` directory: what (clip ids, the speaker refs they carried), when, why, which operator (an opaque id), the request reference, and the counts and file names touched. It holds no name, no transcript and no audio. Nothing is ever rewritten or removed from it. A bank reads it, so a removed clip stays ruled out even if a copy of the file is put back; a line that is not valid JSON stops the bank from loading, because skipping it would bring a clip back.

It is idempotent. Run again, it finds nothing left to change and says so; no second record is written for no work. Run again with a further `--results` directory, it marks that directory too and writes a record of that work. An unfinished removal (a `started` line with no later `complete`: a crash, or an `incomplete` that was never finished) stays open and is reported by every `forget` run, which then exits 1, and it fails `validate --clips`, with the removal id and the command to finish it; only a later `complete` that covers its clips (running `forget` again for that target) clears it. A failure on one file (a read-only file, say) does not stop the rest: the record says `incomplete`, the command prints what failed and exits 1. Fix the cause and run it again to finish. If it is interrupted, run it again: the audio is deleted first, the record is written after the stored files change, and the manifest rows go last.

### What it lists and does not change

- Any other file in a results directory that names the clip (`upload.json`, scorecards, acceptance pages) is listed as "left alone".
- **Recorded calls.** A stored run that played the clip and names a recorded call (`audio` with `caller` and `agent` tracks, written beside the run file by the phone and ElevenLabs legs) has its **caller track deleted**: it holds the clip's voice. The agent track is listed and **not deleted**, as is every other sound file found in the results folders. With `--keep-text` (retention expiry only) the caller track is also kept and listed. Matching is by the paths in the run file, so a recording that merely sits beside the clips is never touched. Carrier and engine recordings stay outside the tool.

### Outside this tool: said again every time it runs

- **Runs uploaded to a dashboard** hold the clip names and the transcript of what the caller said, in the server's database. `forget` cannot change them. Delete those runs on the server, or upload the directory again with `upload --replace` after the results here were handled. A directory with `upload.json` was uploaded.
- The carrier's and the voice engine's own recordings of the calls, and any backup or copy of the bank, scenarios or results made before.
- Source recordings. If the clips were cut with `clipbank build --recordings <dir>`, the whole calls in that directory hold the person's voice and are not touched. `forget` prints their ids (from `source_ref` or `call`) and, given `--recordings <dir>`, lists the matching files. Delete them yourself if the request covers them.
- Anything outside the run result files: uploads, scorecards, acceptance pages and call recordings are listed, never changed.

### On timing

Under UK GDPR an erasure request is answered within one month. The log is how you show that the removal happened and when; the request itself, and who made it, belong in your own records, referred to here only by `--request-ref`.
